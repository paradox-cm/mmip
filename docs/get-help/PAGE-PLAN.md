# Get Help Page — Structure Research & Recommendations

Researched 2026-08-28. Sources studied: StrongHearts Native Helpline (strongheartshelpline.org), NIWRC MMIW Toolkit
(niwrc.org/mmiwtoolkit) and its "When a Loved One Goes Missing" pocket guide (72-hour guide), the federal Operation Lady
Justice family guide (ovc.ojp.gov/library/publications/when-loved-one-goes-missing-...), MMIP San Diego's Get Help page
(mmipsd.org/get-help), Yurok Tribal Court's To' Kee Skuy' Soo Ney-Wo-Chek' program (yuroktribalcourt.org), CA DOJ Office of
Native American Affairs (oag.ca.gov/nativeamerican), UIHI (uihi.org — data/reports, not direct services), CTFC, CHP Feather Alert.

## Patterns across leading MMIP sites

- **Danger triage comes first.** StrongHearts opens with a safety alert (computer monitoring, quick exit); MMIPSD opens with
  "Are you in Danger?" + 911. Every strong page answers "is this an emergency?" before anything else.
- **A numbered when-someone-goes-missing checklist is the centerpiece**, not the directory. NIWRC's core artifact is literally
  titled "What to Do in the First 72 Hours"; MMIPSD uses 8 numbered steps with icons; OVC's guide is organized as a timeline
  (first days → 30+ days → long-term/aftermath).
- **Jurisdiction confusion is treated as the #1 enemy.** NIWRC: "Your missing loved one's location and tribal affiliation will
  determine who to call." Offer a simple who-to-call path instead of explaining jurisdiction law up front.
- **Adults and minors are split immediately** (NIWRC's toolkit excludes under-17 and reroutes to NCMEC/"When Your Child Is Missing").
- **Native-first ordering**: Native-run services (StrongHearts, tribal programs) listed before mainstream equivalents.
- **CA-specific**: Feather Alert gets its own explainer (MMIPSD, Yurok both do this).

## Recommended section order

1. **Emergency banner** (persistent, top): "In immediate danger? Call 911." + quick-exit button and a plain note that there is
   **no waiting period** to report someone missing. Include "Under 18? Call 911, then NCMEC 1-800-843-5678."
2. **Need someone to talk to right now** — 3 pinned cards: StrongHearts (Native-first), 988 call/text, Crisis Text Line.
3. **If your loved one is missing: the first 72 hours** — numbered action checklist (below), printable/downloadable.
4. **Feather Alert (California)** — what it is, criteria, and exactly how families push for activation through local law
   enforcement or their tribe.
5. **Report & register the case** — the escalation ladder: local/tribal police report → ask for NCIC entry → NamUs entry →
   CA DOJ Missing Persons → BIA MMU / FBI if unresponsive or cross-jurisdictional.
6. **Regional directory** — filterable cards (category × county/region), the CMS-driven body of the page.
7. **Support beyond the search** — victim compensation (CalVCB), legal help (CILS), advocacy (CTFC), Native health & healing
   (CRIHB, CCUIH member clinics), 211 referrals, grief/trauma support.
8. **Family toolkit downloads** — NIWRC 72-hour pocket guide, OVC family guide, missing-person flyer template.

## Category taxonomy (keep the planned 6 — confirmed)

The planned six cover everything we verified; a 7th is unnecessary. Recommended slugs + display labels + scope:

- `law-enforcement` — "Emergency & Law Enforcement": 911 guidance, tribal/local PD, BIA MMU, FBI, CA DOJ ONAA.
- `missing-persons` — "Missing Person Reporting & Alerts": NamUs, NCMEC, CA DOJ Missing Persons, Feather Alert, Yurok MMIP program.
- `crisis-hotlines` — "Crisis & Talk Lines": 988, Crisis Text Line, 211 referrals.
- `dv-sa` — "Domestic, Sexual & Trafficking Violence": StrongHearts, NDVH, RAINN, Human Trafficking Hotline (fold trafficking
  here rather than adding a category).
- `native-health` — "Native Health & Healing": CRIHB, CCUIH + member clinics, cultural/traditional healing supports.
- `legal-advocacy` — "Legal Help & Victim Advocacy": CILS, CalVCB, CTFC, family/court advocates.
  Cross-cutting flags, not categories: `native` (Native-led/Native-specific), `24/7`, region. Pin StrongHearts/988 in section 2
  regardless of category so taxonomy never hides a lifeline.

## "First 72 hours" guidance (each claim cited)

1. **Report immediately — there is NO waiting period.** California law requires every police and sheriff's department to accept
   a missing person report "including a report by telephone … without delay," regardless of where the person went missing.
   You never need to wait 24/48 hours, and you don't need proof something is wrong. [oag.ca.gov/missing]
   NIWRC: contact police immediately; "Do not wait to gather more information before taking this critical first step."
   [NIWRC pocket guide, niwrc.org/mmiwtoolkit]
2. **Give a full description**: legal name and nicknames, date of birth, height/weight, hair/eyes, tattoos/scars/birthmarks,
   clothing last worn, vehicle and plate, medical conditions and medications, tribal affiliation. [NIWRC pocket guide]
3. **Write down the report number** plus the officer's name, badge number, and phone — keep a dedicated notebook of every
   contact, date, and promise made. [NIWRC pocket guide]
4. **Ask what systems the case was entered into.** Request entry into NCIC (the FBI's national database) and, once the police
   report exists, create a NamUs profile — families can enter cases themselves at namus.nij.ojp.gov; every case is vetted with
   the investigating agency before it's published, and NamUs provides free forensic services incl. family DNA kits.
   [namus.nij.ojp.gov/frequently-asked-questions; namus.nij.ojp.gov/contact]
5. **Ask about a Feather Alert** if the missing person is Indigenous and may be in danger: law enforcement (or a California
   tribe) requests activation from CHP's ENTAC center; if law enforcement hasn't made a determination within 24 hours of the
   report, the tribe may request activation from CHP directly. Also ask about a BOLO broadcast and any other alert (Amber,
   Silver, Ashanti). [chp.ca.gov/news-alerts/alerts/Feather-Alert; Gov. Code § 8594.13, leginfo.legislature.ca.gov; NIWRC pocket guide]
6. **Gather recent photos and make a flyer**; assign friends/family specific tasks — calling hospitals, jails, shelters,
   transit agencies; posting flyers; monitoring social media. [NIWRC pocket guide; OVC family guide; mmipsd.org/get-help]
7. **Preserve evidence.** Don't clean their room, car, or last-known location; don't delete or alter texts, call history, or
   social media accounts — police may need records. Limit access to the area and photograph it. [NIWRC pocket guide]
8. **If police won't act or the case crosses jurisdictions**: contact tribal police and the county sheriff; email/call CA DOJ
   Missing Persons (missing.persons@doj.ca.gov) or the Office of Native American Affairs (916-210-6474); call the BIA Missing &
   Murdered Unit (1-833-560-2065) or the FBI (1-800-CALL-FBI / tips.fbi.gov). Tribes can go straight to CHP for a Feather
   Alert after 24 hours. [oag.ca.gov/missing; oag.ca.gov/nativeamerican/contact; bia.gov/service/mmu; fbi.gov/contact-us; chp.ca.gov]
9. **Under 18?** Call 911 and NCMEC's 24-hour line, 1-800-843-5678, right away; child cases follow a different path.
   [missingkids.org; NIWRC toolkit note routing minors to separate resources]
10. **Care for the family too**: StrongHearts (1-844-762-8483, 24/7, Native-centered) and 988 for the people searching;
    later, CalVCB victim compensation may cover crime-related costs. [strongheartshelpline.org; 988lifeline.org; victims.ca.gov]

## Tone guidance

- **Plain language, action-first.** Short imperative sentences ("Call now. You do not have to wait."), one action per line,
  ~6th-grade reading level; numbers as digits; every phone number tappable (tel:) and repeated in full each time.
- **Dignity-first.** "Your loved one," never "the subject/case." No blame framing (runaway, lifestyle, substance use, sex
  work); no graphic statistics on the help page itself. Honor Indigenous identity: name tribal sovereignty, use community
  language (relatives, MMIP), keep imagery respectful, no red-hand shock imagery in crisis flows.
- **Non-clinical and jargon-free.** Expand every acronym on first use in one clause ("NamUs, the national missing persons
  database"). Explain systems as what-they-do-for-you, not agency org charts.
- **Trauma-informed structure** (modeled on StrongHearts/MMIPSD): danger triage first, quick-exit, calm neutral colors in
  crisis sections, checklists over paragraphs, printable versions, low-bandwidth friendly for rural/tribal connectivity.
- **Honest about the system.** Acknowledge that families are sometimes dismissed; pair every "call the police" step with the
  no-waiting-period right and a named escalation path — that is what NIWRC, OVC, and MMIPSD all quietly do.
